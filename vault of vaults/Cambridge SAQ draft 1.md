My interest in chemistry led me to explore further in connected topics. I was often surprised by how physics underpinned the workings for several chemistry topics, such as the cell potentials from A level, or the viability of a reaction. While looking at A level reactions such as esterification or dehydration of cyclohexanol I often wondered what determined the viability of a reaction. This then led me to look at Gibbs free energy, which combined entropy and enthalpy to provide a value which could be used to predict the thermodynamic viability of a reaction: if ΔG < 0, then the reaction would be spontaneous, but it wouldn't necessarily be observable due to kinetic factors. It also ties in with the cell potentials, with ΔG = -nFE giving the maximum possible work done by the cell. I was also intrigued by a video on making a YBCO superconductor which involved both chemistry knowledge on how to accurately synthesise the material with the correct proportions and physics in how this chemical makeup would allow the phenomenon of superconductivity to occur. This led to me choosing to research more on this topic when we were tasked in school to pick a physics topic of our choosing and present on it. I initially explored BCS theory as it was the first theory to provide a mechanism for the phenomenon of superconductivity. Reading about the explanation of how the Cooper pairs could occupy the same quantum state since they have integer spins reminded me of the Pauli exclusion principle
