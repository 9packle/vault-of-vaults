I would like to study this course because I have always been interested in the sciences since starting formally, and gradually I came to especially enjoy chemistry from all the interesting experiments and demonstrations. One such example would be the flame colour test for identifying metals. I wanted to know why the red flame would be produced by the presence of lithium, or a blue-green on by the presence of copper. The Bohr model suggested discrete energy levels where the electrons would be in orbitals, and in physics we learnt about how certain wavelengths of light would be released when electrons dropped from one discrete level to another, given by ΔE = hc/λ, and these levels would be different for different elements. This provided me a much clearer chain of reasoning for the different colours emitted using the quantum-mechanical nature of the electrons' transitions, and also showed how physics and maths can be the tools which give predictive power to chemistry, which is where I want to study: in between the lines of just chemistry or physics.
My subject choices of Maths, Further Maths, Physics and Chemistry also tie into my achievements in competitions, which I actively looked for as a way to stretch my understanding of the subjects, both in the paper itself and in the preparation process for the competition during which I pushed past the level the school curriculum required of me. I achieved a Gold in the Senior Maths Challenge twice and subsequently a Merit in the British Maths Olympiad both times. The SMC both forced me to look more for a fast and optimal way to solve the questions as well as be accurate under time pressure, which I worked on by looking at a problem and trying to find as many solutions as possible without time pressure, which helped with being able to spot a more efficient method during the exam. The Olympiad was different again and required me to learn to rigorously structure solutions while ruling out all other possibilities so that by logic the answer provided was the only one. This way of thinking also helped for some organic synthesis questions where I initially wasn't able to work out the next molecule in the step, as I could use a similar way of thinking to eliminate possible answers with the given information until the answer revealed itself.
My interest in Chemistry led me to achieve a Gold in Round 1 of the UK Chemistry Olympiad as well as a Gold twice in C3L6 and throughout the preparation my favourite questions were the organic synthesis questions, since initially I enjoyed the challenge they posed knowing very little of the common reagents and reaction pathways as they were quite a lot more involved than organic chemistry at A level, but I slowly learnt common reagents and types of reactions to be able to work through each step of synthesis pathways. Each piece of information I picked up felt rewarding since often through looking at one type of mechanism, many other scenarios with similar functional groups could also be solved by following the same train of thought. Being able to go through every single structure in a question for the first time felt like a confirmation of my efforts and interests, and made me more certain of my passion for chemistry.
