I would like to study this course because I have always been interested in STEM related subjects since coming into contact with them, and I always enjoy being able to explain to understand phenomena in the real world with digestible chunks of knowledge from the different sciences I studied at school. I especially enjoy chemistry since it can explain everything from an atomic level, to macroscopic observations, such as the structure and behaviour of materials or the mechanisms by which vital processes in our bodies occur, all through both theory dictated by and experimentation.

My subject choices at school have always been focused on maths and the sciences, and my A level choices have further enriched my understanding of Chemistry: Maths and Further Maths have given me the tools to approach difficult questions; Physics has given me additional context on many ideas and theories in Chemistry as well as an appreciation for the connection between the three subjects; Chemistry has taught me the fundamentals concepts and theories importance of experimentation to confirm theory, as well as piquing additional interest in chemistry outside of curriculum. For example, we had a research task during Physics where we were asked to write a 1000-word summary about a relevant topic of our choosing. I chose to do mine on BCS theory and superconductivity since it contains both physical knowledge in its explanation of superconductivity and chemical knowledge in the constituent elements which gives a superconductor its properties, which I was inspired to do after seeing a video on how to make a high-temperature superconductor.
These subject choices also tie into my achievements in competitions, which I actively looked for as a way to stretch my understanding of the subjects, both in the paper itself and in the preparation process for the competition which stretched my knowledge of the subjects. I achieved a Gold in the Senior Maths Challenge twice and subsequently a Merit in the British Maths Olympiad both times. The preparation required me to consider the problems logically and creatively, especially in the Olympiad where it presented questions and required answers significantly different to the style required by the curriculum, making sure that my solutions were rigorously constructed. 
My interest in Chemistry also led me to achieve a Gold with a score of 55 in the Round 1 of the UK Chemistry Olympiad as well as a Gold twice in C3L6 which required me to look extensively at topics outside of the curriculum, especially in understanding the mechanisms and reagents involved in organic synthesis beyond what was required of A level.

Beyond the school curriculum I have also helped with teaching GCSE-level knowledge at another school, which helped me learn valuable skills in communicating with people of different backgrounds and abilities. My interest in chemistry also led me to read more about MO theory in *why chemical reactions happen*. Chapter 4 was especially interesting since it describes the orbitals of electrons as wavefunctions which dictate the probability of finding an electron at any point, and Chapter 5 and 6 illustrated how the breaking and reforming of bonds could be looked at as electrons in bonding and anti-bonding orbitals, which was different to how I looked at reactions before using the A level system of ionic and covalent bonds.
